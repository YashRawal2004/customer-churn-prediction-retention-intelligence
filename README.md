# Customer Churn Prediction & Risk-Based Retention Engine

An end-to-end churn analytics project built on the **Telco Customer Churn** dataset. It goes beyond a plain "will this customer churn" model — it segments customers by risk, explains *why* each customer is at risk using SHAP, and maps that reasoning to a concrete, personalized retention offer.

The project is split into two notebooks that form a pipeline:

| Notebook | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `main.ipynb` | Data cleaning, EDA, feature engineering, model training/selection, threshold tuning |
| `Risk_Segmentation.ipynb` | Loads the trained model, scores customers, segments them by churn risk, explains predictions with SHAP, and generates targeted retention offers |

---

## 1. Problem Statement

Predicting *that* a customer will churn is only half the problem — a business also needs to know **how urgently** to act and **what to do** about it. This project builds that full loop:

1. Predict each customer's probability of churning.
2. Bucket customers into risk segments (`Critical`, `High Risk`, `Medium Risk`, `Low Risk`).
3. Identify the top drivers pushing each individual customer toward churn (via SHAP).
4. Recommend a personalized intervention (offer, channel, discount, urgency) based on the customer's segment and dominant churn driver.

---

## 2. Dataset

**Source:** `WA_Fn-UseC_-Telco-Customer-Churn.csv` (the widely-used IBM Telco Customer Churn dataset).

Each row represents a telecom customer, with fields covering demographics (gender, senior citizen status, partner/dependents), account information (tenure, contract type, payment method, billing), subscribed services (internet, streaming, tech support, security add-ons), charges (monthly/total), and the churn label.

> Note: the raw CSV is not included in this repo — place `WA_Fn-UseC_-Telco-Customer-Churn.csv` in the working directory before running `main.ipynb`.

## 3. Part 1 — `main.ipynb`: Data Prep & Modeling

### Data Cleaning
- Converted `TotalCharges` from string to numeric (11 blank entries corresponded to brand-new customers with `tenure == 0`).
- Verified that for month-to-month customers, `TotalCharges ≈ tenure × MonthlyCharges`, confirming the relationship was safe to rely on.
- Removed the zero-tenure "new customer" rows from the training set (kept aside as a holdout of brand-new sign-ups).
- Checked for duplicates and outliers (z-score) on numeric columns — none found requiring removal.

### Exploratory Data Analysis
- Churn class distribution (imbalanced — churners are the minority class).
- Univariate distributions of numeric features (`tenure`, `MonthlyCharges`, `TotalCharges`, `SeniorCitizen`).
- Churn rate broken down by every categorical feature (bar charts + pie charts).
- Key patterns surfaced:
  - Senior citizens churn at a noticeably higher rate.
  - Customers with **Fiber optic** internet churn more than DSL or no-internet customers.
  - Lack of **Online Security** / **Tech Support** correlates strongly with churn.
  - **Month-to-month** contracts, **paperless billing**, and **electronic check** payment all correlate with higher churn.
  - Most churn happens early in the customer lifecycle (0–30 months tenure), with a smaller secondary group of long-tenured customers also churning.

### Feature Engineering
- One-hot / binary encoding of all categorical variables (`gender`, `MultipleLines`, `InternetService`, `OnlineSecurity`, `OnlineBackup`, `DeviceProtection`, `TechSupport`, `StreamingTV`, `StreamingMovies`, `Contract`, `PaymentMethod`, etc.).
- Two engineered features:
  - **`lifetime_value_proxy`** = `tenure × MonthlyCharges` — a rough proxy for total revenue generated by the customer.
  - **`spend_consistency`** = `TotalCharges / (tenure + 1)` — flags customers whose current spend deviates from their historical spend.

### Modeling
Multiple algorithms were trained and compared, with iterative tuning at each step:

| Model | Notes |
|---|---|
| Decision Tree | Baseline overfit heavily; tuned via `GridSearchCV` (depth, split/leaf sizes, criterion) |
| Random Forest | Tuned with class weighting to balance precision/recall; final model used for downstream segmentation |
| Logistic Regression | Tried with/without feature scaling and L1/L2 regularization via `GridSearchCV` |
| K-Nearest Neighbors | Tuned neighbors/weights/metric; overfit on train, weaker generalization |
| Gradient Boosting | Trained with and without class rebalancing (random undersampling) |

Class imbalance was also addressed via **random undersampling** to test whether rebalancing improved generalization for tree-based models.

**Final model:** a tuned `RandomForestClassifier` (`class_weight={0:1, 1:2}`, `max_depth=7`, `min_samples_leaf=10`), chosen for its balance of accuracy and recall on churners. Decision threshold was tuned (settled around **0.47**, rather than the default 0.5) using precision-recall and ROC curves.

### Outputs
- `trained_model.pkl` — the final serialized Random Forest model.
- `cleaned_data.csv` — the fully cleaned and feature-engineered dataset used for training.

## 4. Part 2 — `Risk_Segmentation.ipynb`: Segmentation & Recommendations

This notebook picks up where `main.ipynb` left off — loading `cleaned_data.csv` and `trained_model.pkl` — and turns raw churn probabilities into actionable, explainable business recommendations.

### Step 1: Score Customers
Each customer is scored with `predict_proba` to get a churn probability.

### Step 2: Risk Segmentation
Customers are bucketed into four segments based on churn probability:

| Segment | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | ≥ 0.70 |
| **High Risk** | ≥ 0.45 |
| **Medium Risk** | ≥ 0.20 |
| **Low Risk** | < 0.20 |

### Step 3: Explainability with SHAP
`shap.TreeExplainer` is used to compute per-customer feature contributions to the churn prediction. For every customer, the **top 3 drivers** pushing their prediction toward churn are extracted (feature name + SHAP value), giving a transparent, case-by-case explanation instead of a black-box score.

### Step 4: Rule-Based Intervention Engine
An `INTERVENTION_RULES` lookup table maps `(segment, top_driver)` pairs to a concrete retention playbook, including:
- **Offer** — the specific retention offer (e.g., free security/tech-support bundle, loyalty discount, auto-pay incentive).
- **Rationale** — why this offer fits this segment/driver combination, grounded in the data (e.g., "83% of Critical customers list tenure as their #1 driver, at an average 4.9 months tenure").
- **Channel** — how to reach the customer (e.g., direct call within 48 hours vs. email/SMS).
- **Discount / cost type** — the economic cost of the intervention.
- **Urgency** — how quickly the business should act.

A robust parser (`get_top_driver`) extracts the #1 driver from the SHAP output regardless of how it was serialized to CSV (quoted, unquoted, or Excel-mangled strings), and `recommend_intervention` applies the rule table across the full customer base.

### Step 5: Reporting & Output
- Summary statistics per segment/offer combination (customer count, average churn probability, average lifetime value, average monthly charge, average discount).
- Coverage check to flag any customers whose `(segment, driver)` pair didn't match a defined rule.
- Final export: **`intervention_recommendations.csv`** — one row per customer with their segment, churn probability, top driver, and recommended offer/channel/discount/urgency/rationale, ready to hand off to a marketing or retention team.

## 8. Key Takeaways

- **Tenure**, lack of **online security/tech support**, **fiber optic internet**, **month-to-month contracts**, and **electronic check payment** are the strongest churn signals across models.
- Churn risk is highly explainable at the individual level — SHAP lets the model justify *why* it flagged a specific customer, which is what makes the intervention rules trustworthy enough to act on.
- Turning a churn score into a **segment + driver + offer** triplet converts a prediction into something a retention team can actually execute against.
